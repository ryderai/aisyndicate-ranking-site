What the numbers say

Four things fell out of the data. The long version, with the evidence, is in Findings.

Finding 01

The cobbler's children have no shoes

Of 29 agencies selling AI search visibility, 1 publishes both AI-readability files on its own website. 15 of 29 have llms.txt. 1 of 29 have agents.md. The average score across the whole index is 32.0 out of 100.

Finding 02

Nobody is talking to the crawlers

We looked for eleven AI crawler names in every robots.txt: GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot, anthropic-ai, CCBot, Google-Extended, Applebot-Extended, Bytespider and Amazonbot. 28 of 29 robots.txt files contain none of them. One agency names an SEO tool's crawler and no AI crawler at all. Another invents a directive no crawler reads.

Finding 03

A missing file that answers anyway is worse than a missing file

Four sites return a normal web page, with a success status, when an AI client asks for a file that does not exist. The client cannot tell the file is missing. A clean 404 is more useful than a page pretending to be an answer.

Finding 04

You cannot buy any of it without talking to somebody

28 of 29 agencies give you no way to start without a sales call. Not one of them will take your money from a button. Several have a button that looks like one — one is literally at the URL /signup — and every one of them lands on a form asking for your phone number and your budget.
